$ file …/src/grouping.ts

The separator is unchanged — it is still `U+0000`, which is the right choice

## 1. The corpus, and why it is generated rather than fixtured

`syntheticCorpus()` is deterministic from a seed (`test/corpora/rng.ts`,
a `mulberry32` — no `Math.random`, so a failure reproduces exactly). It emits
**2,764 inputs across 73 merchants**, of which **43 are gold recurring streams**
and **22 are pure-noise merchants** (30%, the figure 07 §2.12 asks for). The
noise is not decoration: it is the precision denominator, and
`golden-streams.test.ts` asserts `noiseMerchantCount > 0.25 × merchantCount` so
the corpus cannot quietly become easy.

The generator injects fourteen realities. Each row of this table is checked
twice: the count comes from the generator, and
`'every injected reality is observed in the output, not merely generated'`
asserts that the _result_ shows the engine met it — a corpus that injects a card
migration proves nothing if nothing downstream ever mentions one.

| reality                  | injection                                                                   | the engine must                                                                                       |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `WEEKEND_SHIFT` × 36     | a slot landing on Sat/Sun posts on the following Monday, for 4 streams in 5 | score it as tight, not late — tolerance widens forward only                                           |
| `SKIPPED_PERIOD` × 1     | one scheduled occurrence never happens                                      | lose coverage, keep the stream, and not re-anchor                                                     |
| `FAILED_RETRY` × 1       | one occurrence posts three days late (failed payment, retried)              | match it inside tolerance and widen the MAD band by a day, not re-anchor                              |
| `PRICE_HIKE` × 2         | a mid-stream rise of 18–50%; the 50% case splits the amount buckets         | stay one stream, emit one two-sided changepoint, re-baseline the amount                               |
| `DESCRIPTOR_CHANGE` × 1  | the descriptor changes mid-stream while the merchant identity does not      | be unable to tell — the engine sees only `merchantId` (D25), which is the point                       |
| `CARD_MIGRATION` × 1     | the account changes mid-stream after a one-period gap (card reissued)       | keep one stream, propose `CARD_MIGRATION`, and not call it a cancellation                             |
| `PRORATION` × 1          | one charge at a third of the usual amount, then the old amount resumes      | not call it a price change (both-sides stability fails)                                               |
| `ANNUAL_TO_MONTHLY` × 2  | an annual plan stops and a monthly plan at the same merchant begins         | produce two streams, not one merged nonsense cadence                                                  |
| `REFUND` × 1             | an inflow from a merchant that also bills the account                       | not type it `RECURRING_INCOME` (sign split, then the refund suppression)                              |
| `DUPLICATE_CHARGE` × 1   | the same charge twice on the same day                                       | keep both ids, count the date once, flag the duplicate                                                |
| `DORMANCY` × 2           | two streams stop four to seven months before "today"                        | climb the ladder to `LIKELY_CANCELED`/`CANCELED` and stop forecasting                                 |
| `NOISE_MERCHANT` × 22    | 30% of merchants are pure noise: 1–6 unrelated purchases                    | detect nothing — this is the precision denominator                                                    |
| `AMBIGUOUS_MERCHANT` × 5 | three charges a month apart at varying amounts, from an unclassified payee  | be genuinely uncertain — these are the corpus's hard negatives and what the sweep is choosing against |
| `HABIT_MERCHANT` × 4     | coffee and groceries, 2–3 times a week at varying amounts                   | type `FREQUENT_MERCHANT`, never a subscription                                                        |

**What the corpus does not contain, and therefore does not prove.** It is
synthetic. It has no real Plaid data, no multi-currency household, no merchant
that genuinely changes billing platform, and its noise is drawn from a
distribution somebody chose. The phase-2 exit criterion is P/R against _Chris's
confirmed list_; this corpus is the regression gate that stops the engine
getting worse between now and then, not a substitute for it. That carry-over is
already on the books in the dispatch plan (§2, "two real institutions").

## 2. The P/R curve and the operating point

Matching rule, 07 §2.12 verbatim: a predicted stream `P` matches a gold stream
`G` iff the merchant matches **and** `Jaccard(txn_ids(P), txn_ids(G)) ≥ 0.5`.
Matching on merchant alone would let a detector that shattered one subscription
into four quarters score four matches; the Jaccard floor makes "found it" mean
"found most of it".

The threshold is **swept, not asserted**. `precisionRecallCurve` walks
confidence from 0.50 to 0.95 in 0.01 steps and `operatingPoint` takes 07 §2.12's
rule — _the highest recall at which precision is still ≥ 0.95_. The gate then
asserts the targets at whatever point that sweep lands on. A test that hard-codes
a threshold and checks precision at it measures whether somebody remembered to
update a constant.

| threshold | precision | recall    | predicted | matched |
| --------- | --------- | --------- | --------- | ------- |
| 0.50      | 0.782     | 1.000     | 55        | 43      |
| 0.55      | 0.782     | 1.000     | 55        | 43      |
| 0.60      | 0.782     | 1.000     | 55        | 43      |
| 0.65      | 0.811     | 1.000     | 53        | 43      |
| 0.70      | 0.811     | 1.000     | 53        | 43      |
| 0.75      | 0.915     | 1.000     | 47        | 43      |
| 0.80      | 0.915     | 1.000     | 47        | 43      |
| 0.85      | 0.915     | 1.000     | 47        | 43      |
| **0.93**  | **1.000** | **0.977** | **42**    | **42**  |
| 0.95      | 1.000     | 0.977     | 42        | 42      |

**Operating point: threshold 0.93, precision 1.000, recall 0.977.**

### Why precision is weighted above recall

07 §2.12 says so, and the dispatch plan's risk table gives the reason in one
line: _a false subscription destroys trust faster than a missed one_. The
asymmetry is not a preference, it is a property of what the two errors cost.

- A **false positive** is an unprompted claim about the user's money that is
  wrong. It arrives as a notification, a budget line and a price-change alert.
  The user cannot verify it cheaply — they have to go and check — and once one
  has been wrong, every later one is suspect, including the correct ones. The
  damage is to the whole feed, not to one row.
- A **false negative** is silence. The user notices the subscription in their
  statement, as they would have anyway, and the candidate turns up later when a
  third or fourth charge arrives. The cost is a delay, and it is bounded.

The two are also not symmetric in _recoverability_: WP 2.3's pipeline gives a
human a confirm/reject on every candidate, so a miss is recovered by the next
detection pass, while a false positive has already been shown before anybody can
reject it. That is why the surfacing bar sits at 0.93 rather than at
`CANDIDATE_CONFIDENCE` (0.55) or `AUTO_CONFIRM_CONFIDENCE` (0.80): those two
constants govern _what the pipeline does with a candidate_, and this one governs
_whether the corpus is allowed to call it a detection at all_.

The sweep is choosing rather than confirming: at the bottom of the curve
precision is 0.782, well under target, and the gate asserts that too
(`curve[0].precision < 0.95`). 12 of the 55 streams at threshold 0.50 are wrong;
by 0.93 all 12 are gone and exactly one true stream goes with them.

### The single recall miss, named

`missed: s07/mer-0007` — and it is the `CARD_MIGRATION` stream, missed **by
design**:

```
gold s07  merchant=mer-0007 txns=35 cadence=MONTHLY
stream    mer-0007:OUTFLOW:USD:0:2023-10-03  n=35  type=SUBSCRIPTION
          cadence=MONTHLY/0.948  accounts=acc-visa,acc-visa-reissued
          merge=CARD_MIGRATION  jaccard=1.000  confidence=0.889  surfaced=false
```

The engine found it perfectly: Jaccard 1.000, both accounts, one stream, not a
cancellation-plus-new-subscription. It then applied 07 §2.2's discount for a
_proposed, unconfirmed_ merge — `confidence *= 0.9` — which takes 0.988 to
0.889, just under the 0.93 bar. That is the precision-first policy doing exactly
what it is for: a cross-account merge is the engine's most speculative claim, so
it waits for a human instead of asserting it. Recall 0.977 is what that costs,
and it is well above the 0.85 target.

## 3. The eight metrics of 07 §2.12

At the swept operating point (threshold 0.93), from
`test/corpora/metrics.ts` via `report.ts`. Targets are 07 §2.12's.

| #   | metric (07 §2.12)               | target                       | measured                                   | asserted in                                                    |
| --- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | stream precision                | ≥ 0.95                       | **1.000**                                  | `'precision ≥ 0.95 and recall ≥ 0.85 on the synthetic corpus'` |
| 2   | stream recall                   | ≥ 0.85                       | **0.977**                                  | same                                                           |
| 3   | cadence accuracy                | ≥ 0.92                       | **1.000**                                  | `'the remaining 07 §2.12 metrics hold at the operating point'` |
| 4   | next-due MAE (days)             | ≤ 2.0 monthly / ≤ 5.0 annual | **0.25 / 1.25** (all cadences 0.38)        | same                                                           |
| 5   | amount MAPE (FIXED streams)     | ≤ 1%                         | **0.00%**                                  | same                                                           |
| 6   | price-change precision / recall | ≥ 0.90 / ≥ 0.85              | **1.000 / 1.000**                          | same                                                           |
| 7   | cancellation lead time          | ≤ 1.5 periods                | **`AT_RISK` 1.05**; `LIKELY_CANCELED` 1.67 | same                                                           |
| 8   | trial-conversion recall         | ≥ 0.80                       | **not implemented**                        | —                                                              |

Two rows need their footnote said out loud rather than buried.

**#7 is met at `AT_RISK`, not at `LIKELY_CANCELED`.** 07 §2.8's ladder cannot
reach `LIKELY_CANCELED` inside 1.5 periods: it requires two missed periods
_plus_ grace, so 1.67 periods is the arithmetic minimum and no implementation
can do better without changing the ladder. `AT_RISK` at 1.05 periods is the
first rung the user is actually shown ("we haven't seen a charge since…"), and
it is the one measured against the target. Lead time is measured honestly, by
re-running the whole engine on that merchant's rows at weekly steps with a
different clock — the engine is pure, so "what would it have said last Tuesday?"
is just another call.

**#8 is not implemented.** 07 §2.7's free-trial conversion needs a seeded
standard-price table (what a service _normally_ costs) that does not exist in
this codebase. Rather than score 0.00 against a target, it is left to the
catalogue work in WP 2.7, where the table has an owner. Recording it as "not
implemented" rather than omitting the row is deliberate: an eight-metric table
with seven rows is how a missing metric becomes invisible.

Notes for WP 2.3's schema:

- `streamKey` is the natural key for `recurring_series`; `transactionId` for
  `recurring_occurrences`. Both are stable across re-runs over the same history.
- `suppressions` is _not_ diagnostics. It is the answer to "why is my mortgage
  not listed as a subscription?", so it needs to survive into the read model.
- `mergeProposal` must be persisted as a **proposal**, not applied. The whole
  point of 07 §2.2 is that the merge is offered before a cancellation is
  claimed; §2 above shows this is the one thing that costs recall.
- `confidence`, `cadenceScore`, `amountCv` and `coverage` are floats by
  construction and must never reach a `*_minor` column. Every amount on the way
  out (`typicalAmount`, `amountMad`, `delta`, `annualImpact`) is `Money`.

## 5. D25: the engine cannot see a descriptor

`test/no-merchant-writes.test.ts` asserts it three ways, because each is
individually dodgeable:

1. **The exported surface** — no exported function takes a parameter named for a
   descriptor, payee, merchant name, merchant key, alias or trigram, and no
   exported function is named `*createMerchant*`/`*writeMerchant*`/etc. Node
   strips types in place, so parameter _names_ survive `Function.prototype
.toString` while annotations become whitespace, which is the part this needs.
   Dodgeable by renaming the field.
2. **The source** — no identifier under `src/` matches any banned word outside
   comments and string literals (they are stripped before matching, because
   prose may discuss what code may not do). Dodgeable by computing the property
   name.
3. **The behaviour** — a whole detection pass runs with all 24 inputs behind a
   `Proxy` whose `get` trap throws on any key outside the contract, and the
   targets carry three _physically present_ decoys: `descriptor`, `payee`,
   `merchantName`. Nothing dodges this: reading the descriptor fails the test
   even if the code that does it was generated at runtime. The test then asserts
   the pass did real work (streams exist, at least one is not `ONE_OFF`) so a
   green result is not an empty one, and greps the serialised output for the
   decoy text on the way back out.

WP 2.3 carries the other half — `'a full detection pass leaves merchants row
count and ids unchanged'` — because only WP 2.3 has a database to leave
unchanged.

## 6. Sabotage: inverting the cadence tie-break

This section is the one the gate standard §5.2 exists for, and the first attempt
at it **failed in the way the Phase 1 gate predicts**. It is recorded here in
full rather than replaced with the tidy final result.

### 6a. First attempt: the sabotage was invisible

07 §2.3's tie-break — among cadences scoring within `TIE_BAND` (0.05) of the
best, prefer the **longest** period — was inverted in `src/cadence.ts` and the
suite re-run:

```
$ pnpm exec vitest run --project recurring
 Test Files  6 passed (6)
      Tests  35 passed (35)
```

**All green.** The control had never been observed failing, and the docstring on
`inferCadence` claimed the opposite in writing: _"Inverting this one comparison
is the sabotage recorded in the evidence file: it takes the golden corpus from
precision 1.00 to below the gate."_ That sentence was false. Regenerating the
corpus report with the tie-break inverted gives an identical P/R curve, an
identical operating point and identical §2.12 metrics.

Two reasons, both worth having found:

- The test _named_ for the rule — `'the longest cadence wins a near-tie, and
only a near-tie'` — generated clean monthly streams, which are a near-tie with
  nothing. It exercised the argmax and never the tie-break. It was only its
  second half.
- The corpus-level metrics are genuinely insensitive to it. The tie-break only
  decides sparse, ambiguous streams, and those are already held under the
  operating point by the `EARLY_DETECTION` cap at n=2 (0.70 ≪ 0.93). The defence
  against "three monthly hits look like a sparse weekly schedule" is not the
  tie-break at all — it is the coverage term plus per-cadence `min_n` in
  `scoreCadence`, which separate monthly from weekly by more than 0.2, four
  tie-bands.

### 6b. What the tie-break is actually for, measured

Not a precision defence: a _cost_ defence. Two unrelated purchases six weeks
apart tie four ways at 0.750 — quarterly, semiannual and annual explain that
pair exactly as well as each other, and no scoring function can separate them
from two rows. Preferring the longest means the engine promises **one charge a
year** rather than four out of evidence it cannot explain, so the forward
cash-flow it feeds in WP 2.3 over-commits by the smallest amount the evidence
allows.

On the golden corpus, from `report.ts`:

```
tie-break: 11/110 streams change cadence when it is inverted; 4 of those score
>= 0.70; promised charges/year for those streams 11 -> 140 (12.7x)
```

Every one of the 11 moves to a _shorter_ period when the rule is inverted, and
the forward commitment those streams imply grows **12.7×**.

### 6c. The repair: two gates that do go red

- `src/cadence.test.ts` → `'the longest cadence wins a near-tie, and only a
near-tie'` rewritten in three parts: (a) a `fast-check` property over 400 runs
  of sparse, irregular date sets asserting the invariant **on the default path**
  — the chosen cadence's `periodDays` equals the maximum over everything within
  `TIE_BAND`, stated on `considered`, which is the same candidate list the
  tie-break reduces over; (b) a concrete live tie (two dates six weeks apart →
  `ANNUAL` by default, `QUARTERLY` inverted, scores within the band); (c) the
  original "only a near-tie" half, unchanged.
- `test/golden-streams.test.ts` → `'the cadence tie-break decides real streams,
and always towards the longer'`: runs the corpus both ways, requires that more
  than five streams actually change (so it cannot pass vacuously), asserts every
  change goes towards the longer period, and asserts the promised charges per
  year are more than 3× higher inverted.
