## The rule

Every debt is kept current at its minimum payment first. Any money beyond the minimums then goes to one debt at a time, and when that debt is gone its payment rolls on to the next.

Two orders are common. The highest-rate order, often called the avalanche, sends the extra money to the debt with the highest interest rate first, because that balance is costing the most[^cfpb-reduce-debt]. The smallest-balance order, often called the snowball, sends it to the smallest balance first; whole accounts disappear sooner, and the CFPB notes it can mean paying more in the long run[^cfpb-reduce-debt].

PersonalFi's payoff-order finding names, from the holder's own accounts, which balance carries the highest rate and which is the smallest, and states the trade-off between the two orders in words.

## Why

Interest accrues on each balance at that debt's own rate. A dollar of extra principal therefore saves the most interest where the rate is highest, and with every minimum kept, ordering by rate is the order that pays the least interest overall. The minimums come first because a missed payment brings late fees and damage to the credit report that can outweigh any difference in interest between the two orders.

The case for the smallest balance first is behavioural, and it is real: some people keep going because of early, visible wins, and a plan that is followed beats a cheaper plan that is abandoned. The cost of that choice is the extra interest, and it is worth weighing knowingly rather than by default.

## When it does not apply

- **A promotional rate is ending.** A balance at a temporary low rate with a known end date may deserve priority before that date even though its current rate is the lowest; the app's promotional-rate finding states the date and the rate that follows.
- **There is no cushion at all.** If every spare dollar goes to debt, the next surprise expense goes back on a card. One common approach keeps a small starter emergency fund first; the emergency-fund page covers it.
- **Low-rate debt against investing.** Extra payments on a mortgage or a low-rate loan earn a certain return equal to the rate, while investing earns an uncertain expected return. That is a separate trade-off; the app states the loan's rate as the certain side and names the investment return as uncertain, without inventing a figure for it.
- **Special terms.** Federal student loans with income-driven repayment or forgiveness, and debts in collection or in court, follow their own rules; a nonprofit credit counselor or another qualified professional is worth consulting for those.
